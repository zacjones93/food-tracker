# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Food Tracker is for individuals, couples, and households who plan meals together, maintain a trusted recipe collection, and turn a weekly plan into a practical grocery list. They often use it in short bursts in the kitchen, at the store, or while planning on a couch, sometimes with poor connectivity and only one free hand. Their primary job is to decide what to cook, capture or improve a recipe, schedule it, and keep the resulting shopping work accurate across devices.

## Product Purpose

Food Tracker turns a personal recipe library into an executable weekly food plan. Recipes, recipe books, schedules, grocery templates, and grocery items should behave like one coherent system rather than separate databases. Success means a household can make changes quickly on web or mobile, trust that offline work is safe, and return later without reconciling duplicate or stale information by hand.

## Brand Personality

Warm, capable, and quietly distinctive. The product should feel like a well-used recipe binder refined into a dependable tool: welcoming enough for home life, precise enough to trust at the grocery store, and calm enough to use every day. Copy is direct and human, with culinary character used sparingly rather than as decoration.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards made from interchangeable nested cards and oversized summary metrics.
- Purple-gradient, glassmorphic AI interfaces that make ordinary actions feel theatrical.
- Recipe blogs that bury the task under promotional content, lifestyle photography, or excessive prose.
- Desktop tables compressed onto a phone instead of being adapted to touch-first workflows.
- Novel controls, hidden gestures, and ornamental motion that compete with planning or shopping.

## Design Principles

- **From list to action.** Each screen should make the next real-world step obvious, whether that is choosing a meal, adding ingredients, or checking an item at the store.
- **Offline is ordinary.** Local edits, pending sync, conflicts, and recovery are first-class states, not exceptional technical messages.
- **One household truth.** Related recipes, schedules, and grocery items should remain visibly connected and use consistent nouns across platforms.
- **Warmth through restraint.** Character comes from the cream and mystic palette, thoughtful type, and helpful copy, not decorative effects.
- **Earned familiarity.** Use native, accessible interaction patterns so repeat workflows become faster without instruction.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA on the web and the equivalent Apple accessibility guidance on iOS. All primary mobile touch targets are at least 44 by 44 points. The interface supports Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, keyboard navigation, visible focus, sufficient non-color state cues, color-vision-safe status communication, and reduced motion. Grocery and schedule workflows must remain usable outdoors, one-handed, and under low-connectivity conditions.
